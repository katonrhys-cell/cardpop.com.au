Where to Sell Pokémon Cards in Australia (2026): Fees Compared

If you want the most money in your pocket when selling Pokémon cards in Australia, the platform you choose matters more than almost anything else. The same card can net you 10% more or less depending purely on selling fees, payment processing, and how easy buyers find you. This guide breaks down every realistic option for Australian sellers in 2026 — the fees, the trade-offs, and which to pick for your situation.

The short answer

For most Australian collectors, a dedicated local marketplace gives you the best net return: lower fees than eBay, buyers who actually want trading cards, and no international shipping or currency headaches. eBay still has the largest raw audience, and Facebook groups are fee-free but high-effort and higher-risk. The right choice depends on the card's value and how much hands-on work you're willing to do.

Your options, compared

1. CardPop — Australia's dedicated card marketplace

CardPop charges a 3% selling fee, versus eBay's effective ~13.4% once final-value fees and payment processing are included. On a $500 card, that's roughly $15 in fees instead of about $67 — a difference of more than $50 that stays with you. Because every buyer is there specifically for trading cards, listings reach a more relevant audience, and you sell in AUD to Australian buyers with domestic postage. Listing is free.

Best for: singles, graded cards and sealed product where the fee saving is meaningful and you want card-focused buyers.

2. eBay Australia

eBay has the biggest audience and strong buyer trust, but the costs add up: final-value fees plus payment processing land around 13.4% for most categories, and promoted-listing fees climb higher if you want visibility. It works, but on higher-value cards the fee drag is significant, and you're competing against the entire global catalogue for attention.

Best for: hard-to-shift items where raw reach matters more than fee efficiency.

3. Facebook groups & Marketplace

No selling fees, which is the obvious appeal — but you trade that for effort and risk. You handle every message, negotiation, payment and dispute yourself, with no built-in buyer or seller protection. PayID and bank transfers are common, and scams are a real concern for both sides.

Best for: low-value bulk lots, or trusted local trades where you can meet in person.

The fees that quietly eat your profit

  • Final-value / selling fee — the headline percentage the platform takes on sale price.
  • Payment processing — often bundled into the platform fee, but always there.
  • Promotion / ad fees — optional on eBay, but frequently needed to be seen.
  • Postage — domestic only on a local marketplace; potentially international (with customs hassle) on global platforms.

Always compare the effective total, not the advertised headline rate. A "no fees" channel can cost you more in time, risk and lost sales than a 3% marketplace.

How to get the best price (whatever platform you pick)

  • Photograph well: sharp, well-lit images of the front and back, plus close-ups of corners and edges. Most disputes start with bad photos.
  • Price from real sold data: use recently sold listings (not asking prices) as your benchmark, and price in AUD for Australian buyers.
  • State condition honestly: for raw cards, describe edges, centring and surface; for graded cards, list the grader and cert number.
  • Consider grading high-value cards first: a strong PSA grade can lift value well beyond the grading cost — but only on cards likely to grade well.
  • Pack to survive Australia Post: penny sleeve + top loader (or a graded slab protector) in a rigid mailer. A bent card on arrival means a refund.

So, where should you sell?

If you want the highest net return with card-focused buyers and minimal fuss, a dedicated Australian marketplace like CardPop is the strongest default — especially for singles, graded cards and sealed product, where the fee gap is real money. Keep eBay in mind for unusual items that need maximum reach, and use Facebook only for low-value lots or in-person trades where you accept the added risk. Whatever you choose, compare the true all-in cost and protect your cards in transit — that's where most of your profit is won or lost.
